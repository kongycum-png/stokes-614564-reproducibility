The non-monotonic residual at $\eta=0.35$ is resolved by extending the finite-radius event expansion. For $m=3$, independent positive-source quadrature gives $R_0\Delta=4.28613737$, $4.27492522$ and $4.29986839$ at $R_0=24,40,96$. An Airy convolution and angular Fourier identity reproduces the complete reduced R-S signal without an arbitrary spectral partition. The negative-source extension used in this identity has an absolute source-integral bound below $1.16\times10^{-36}$ at $R_0=24$.

The non-fitted fifth- and sixth-order event coefficients are $c_5=3.36680300$ and $c_6=13.35298604$, with $\Delta=\sum_n c_nR_0^{-n/2}$. The minimum of $R_0\Delta$ moves from $R_0=12.91361$ at fourth order to $34.41707$ at sixth order and $36.37128$ at fourteenth order, compared with $36.37130$ for the full reduced R-S calculation. These terms combine coherent field corrections, intensity cross products and the nonlinear peak and crossing conditions. The selected event remains on the same resolved first-peak branch over the tested radii. New-radius predictions and independent arbitrary precision checks are given in Supplementary Material, Sec. 14 and Fig. S7. The finite-order event accuracy is established numerically; the analytic integral identity does not by itself certify a unique global minimum for all radii.
